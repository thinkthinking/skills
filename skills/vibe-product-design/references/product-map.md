# The Complete Product Map — dimensions & diagnostic questions

This is the backbone that guarantees no gaps. Eight layers, each with its
dimensions and the sharp questions a top-tier PM would actually ask. Use it two
ways:

- **As a checklist** — before you tell the user a layer is solid, confirm every
  dimension below is either ✅ addressed or consciously deferred (not silently
  skipped).
- **As a question bank** — when a dimension is ❌ or 🟡, pull the diagnostic
  questions to probe it. Ask one good question at a time, not the whole list.

Every dimension maps to a row in `coverage-map.md`. The IDs (e.g. `0.2`,
`5.3`) are stable handles you can reference in the brief and the decision log.

A note on tone for all questions below: these are written sharp on purpose. In
conversation, soften the delivery to match the user and the moment — the *edge*
should be in the thinking, not in how you treat the person.

---

## Layer 0 — Premise (the foundation almost everyone skips)

Most products are weak here and nowhere else. If this layer is hollow, no amount
of feature polish saves the product. Spend real time here.

- **0.1 The real problem.** What problem, precisely? Whose problem? How do they
  experience it today? *"如果用一句话说清你在解决的问题，是什么？谁会因为这个问题
  半夜睡不着？"* Push past the first answer — the first problem stated is usually
  a symptom, not the root.
- **0.2 Why now.** What changed in the world that makes this possible or urgent
  *now* and not three years ago? (New tech, new behavior, new regulation, new
  cost curve.) A product with no "why now" is usually either too early or a
  vitamin nobody needs. *"为什么是现在？三年前为什么不行，三年后会不会太晚？"*
- **0.3 First-principles check.** Strip the problem to what is physically /
  economically / mathematically true, ignoring how everyone currently solves it.
  What does the solution *have* to look like if you reason up from bedrock rather
  than by analogy to existing products? *"先别管别人怎么做。从最底层的事实出发，这
  件事本来应该怎么解决？"*
- **0.4 Vision.** If this wins completely in 5–10 years, what does the world look
  like? Is the destination big enough to be worth the journey? *"如果彻底成功，
  十年后世界有什么不同？"*
- **0.5 Why you / unfair right to win.** Why is this team the one to do it? Unique
  insight, unfair access, hard-won skill, obsessive founder-market fit? *"凭什么
  是你们？你们知道什么别人不知道、或做得到别人做不到的事？"*
- **0.6 Is it a painkiller or a vitamin?** Honestly: must-have or nice-to-have?
  Vitamins can win but need a different playbook (habit, distribution) than
  painkillers. Name which one this is.

## Layer 1 — People (who it's for)

- **1.1 Target user.** Who *specifically*? Resist "everyone" — the broader the
  claimed user, the weaker the product. *"如果只能服务一类人，是谁？"*
- **1.2 Persona.** Demographics, context, technical comfort, day in their life,
  what they currently use. Concrete enough to picture one real person.
- **1.3 Beachhead / earlyvangelists.** Who are the first 10–100 users who feel the
  pain so acutely they'll tolerate a rough v1 and tell you the truth? Where do
  they cluster? *"谁的痛到了愿意忍受一个粗糙版本、还会主动给你反馈的程度？"*
- **1.4 Pain intensity × frequency.** For each core pain: how *severe* and how
  *often*? Severe-and-frequent is gold; mild-and-rare is a trap. Rank them.
- **1.5 Jobs To Be Done.** What job is the user "hiring" this product to do —
  functional, emotional, and social? What do they fire to hire you? *"用户其实
  是雇你的产品来完成什么任务？他们之前雇的是什么？"*
- **1.6 Willingness to pay / act.** Is the pain worth paying for (money, time,
  behavior change)? Who feels the pain vs. who holds the budget — same person or
  not?

## Layer 2 — Landscape (the world it enters)

- **2.1 Market size — honestly.** Rough TAM/SAM/SOM or a bottom-up estimate. **If
  you don't have real numbers, say so and show the back-of-envelope logic — never
  fabricate a figure.** Mark ⚠️ until grounded. *"先别编市场规模。我们能不能从
  '多少人 × 多少钱 × 多大比例' 自底向上估一个？"*
- **2.2 Alternatives — including "do nothing."** How is the job solved today?
  Always include the status-quo / spreadsheet / "they just live with it" option —
  it's usually the real competitor. *"如果你的产品不存在，他们现在怎么办？"*
- **2.3 Competitors.** Name actual products. Position them; find the gap they
  structurally cannot or will not fill. Don't list features — find the wedge.
- **2.4 Trends / tailwinds.** What macro, tech, or behavioral waves push for or
  against this over the next few years?
- **2.5 Category.** Are you entering an existing category (compete on better) or
  creating one (compete on different/new)? They demand different strategies.

## Layer 3 — The Bet (strategy & how you win)

- **3.1 Unique value proposition.** One sharp sentence: for [user] who [need],
  this is [what] that [unique benefit] — unlike [alternative]. If it sounds like
  everyone else's, it's not a UVP yet. *"用一句话，说清你和现有方案到底有什么不同
  —— 而且这个不同用户真的在乎。"*
- **3.2 Strategy / how you win.** Not a goal ("become #1") — a *theory* of how you
  get from here to winning. What's the wedge, the sequence, the compounding loop?
- **3.3 Focus & explicit non-goals.** The single most valuable PM act. What are
  you deliberately *not* doing, not building, not serving — so the core gets to
  excellent? *"为了把这一件事做到极致，我们明确决定不做哪些事？"* A product with no
  non-goals has no focus.
- **3.4 Moat / defensibility.** Once it works, why won't a bigger/faster player
  copy it to death? Network effects, switching costs, data, brand, economies of
  scale, counter-positioning? Or is there honestly no moat yet (fine for now, but
  name it)? *"做成之后，别人抄你最难抄的是什么？"*
- **3.5 North Star metric.** The *one* number that best captures delivered value
  and, if it goes up, the business wins. Not a vanity metric. *"哪一个数字涨了，
  就说明你在为用户创造真实价值、生意也成立？"*

## Layer 4 — Experience (the solution as the user feels it)

- **4.1 Solution concept.** The shape of the answer — before features. What *is*
  it, in one breath?
- **4.2 Core flow / user journey.** The main path from trigger to value. Where's
  the friction? Where do users drop? (Diagram it — see `output-formats.md`.)
- **4.3 Product principles / taste.** The 3–5 opinions that decide a hundred small
  design questions for you. ("Defaults over settings." "Never make the user
  wait without telling them why.") *"有哪几条原则，定了之后无数小决策就不用再吵了？"*
- **4.4 Information architecture.** The main objects, their relationships, and how
  the user navigates them. Often where hidden complexity lives.
- **4.5 The magic moment.** The single moment the user first goes "oh, wow — I get
  it." When does it happen, how fast can you get them there, and is it strong
  enough to drive word of mouth? *"用户第一次'啊哈'是哪一刻？能不能更快、更强？"*
  If there is no such moment, that is the product's biggest gap.
- **4.6 MVP definition.** The smallest thing that delivers the core value and
  tests the riskiest assumption — not "version 1 with fewer features." *"能验证
  核心假设的最小东西是什么？它必须让用户体验到那个'啊哈'。"*

## Layer 5 — The Build (specification)

This is where the original meta-prompt's craft lives. Preserve its rigor.

- **5.1 Functional architecture.** Modules and how they fit. (Mermaid block
  diagram.)
- **5.2 Feature list.** The structured spec: L1 module → L2 feature group → L3
  feature point → L4 user story, each with priority / effort / complexity. Full
  format in `output-formats.md` and `feature-list.template.md`.
- **5.3 Prioritization.** P0–P3 by value × cost × dependency. Tie priority back to
  Layer 0–3 — a P0 feature must serve the core problem and the bet, or it isn't
  really P0.
- **5.4 Roadmap.** MVP → next → later, framed as hypotheses each release tests,
  not just a feature delivery schedule.
- **5.5 Business / system process flows.** Key end-to-end flows across roles and
  systems, including the unhappy paths (errors, edge cases, rollback). Swimlane
  diagrams.

## Layer 6 — The Engine (business & distribution)

The original workflow underweighted distribution. A product nobody can find is a
failed product — give this layer equal seriousness to the build.

- **6.1 Business model.** How value is created, delivered, and captured. Who pays,
  for what, why.
- **6.2 Revenue streams & pricing.** Model (subscription, usage, transaction,
  ads…), price points, and the *logic* behind the price (value-based, not
  cost-plus). *"用户为这个价值愿意付多少？定价背后的逻辑是什么？"*
- **6.3 Unit economics.** Per-customer or per-transaction: does it make money?
  Rough CAC, LTV, gross margin, payback. Even a sketch beats nothing. *"做一单
  / 一个用户，到底赚钱还是赔钱？"*
- **6.4 Cost structure.** Main fixed and variable costs; what scales sub-linearly.
- **6.5 Distribution / GTM.** **How do users actually discover and adopt this?**
  Which 1–2 channels, why those, and is the channel economically viable at the
  product's price point? *"第一批用户从哪来？这个渠道能规模化吗？算得过账吗？"*
- **6.6 Growth loop / flywheel.** Is there a loop where using the product drives
  more usage or more users (virality, content, data, network)? Or is growth
  purely paid (fine, but know it)?
- **6.7 Key business metrics.** Acquisition, activation, retention, revenue,
  referral — the few that matter, plus how you'd instrument them.

## Layer 7 — The Risks (what kills it)

The original had scattered "validation plans" but no consolidated risk view. This
is where intellectual honesty pays off most.

- **7.1 Riskiest assumptions, ranked.** List everything that must be true for this
  to work. Rank by (likelihood it's false) × (damage if false). The top one is
  where validation effort goes *first*. *"哪个假设最可能是错的、错了最致命？"*
- **7.2 Validation plan for the top assumptions.** For each top risk: the cheapest
  experiment that could prove it false, the metric, and the kill/continue
  threshold *set in advance*.
- **7.3 Pre-mortem.** Imagine it's 18 months later and the product failed.
  Plausibly, why? Work backward from each cause to what you'd do now. *"假设
  18 个月后它失败了，最可能的死因是什么？"*
- **7.4 Dependencies & constraints.** External dependencies (platforms, APIs,
  partners, regulation), and hard constraints (budget, team, time, tech).
- **7.5 Second-order effects & ethics.** If this succeeds at scale, what breaks,
  who's harmed, what's the abuse case, what incentive does it create? Worth a
  sober look even for benign products.
- **7.6 Open questions.** The honest list of what's still unknown — the agenda for
  the next session. A healthy brief always has some.

---

## Using this map well

- **Don't interrogate linearly.** Follow the user's energy; use the map to ensure
  you eventually close every dimension, not to force march through 40 questions.
- **Depth beats breadth.** One dimension probed to bedrock teaches more than ten
  skimmed. The coverage map is what protects breadth, so you're free to go deep.
- **Foundational gaps outrank cosmetic ones.** A ❌ on 0.1 (real problem) matters
  far more than a 🟡 on 5.4 (roadmap detail). Triage accordingly.
- **Connect layers.** The best insights are cross-layer: a Layer 6 pricing reality
  that kills a Layer 5 feature; a Layer 1 user truth that reframes the Layer 3
  bet. Name those connections — they're where the real thinking happens.
